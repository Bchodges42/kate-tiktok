# What this account likes and rejects

Collected from feedback on earlier edits. A direction given for the current edit wins over anything here. Add a line when the user gives new feedback that will apply to later edits.

## The look to aim for

Chic, calm, Pinterest mood board. It should look like a person with taste laid real photos out by hand, then timed them to music. Not animated, not decorated for its own sake, not a slideshow template.

## Liked

- **Whole photos, centred, as big as the screen allows.** Spacing may be tight if it makes photos bigger.
- **Thin borders.** White or cream, a few pixels.
- **Cream and off-white backgrounds** (`#F4ECDA`, `#F7F4EE`), and deep or dusty colours pulled from the board (`#570B28` burgundy, `#93ABBE` dusty blue, slate blue).
- **Overlapping styles**: a centred tilted stack, or a scattered pile, with thin borders and a soft shadow. A cover is never smaller than what it covers. Asked for as "one continuous pile": photos keep landing on top of one another without the screen clearing, starting and ending on a photo in the centre.
- **Neutral backgrounds**: oat `#E9E1D3`, warm taupe `#B9AE9F`.
- **Realistic polaroid frames** with a gentle film look and a blank bottom edge.
- **The dusty blue page with white line doodles** and a stitched border, as a background for polaroid piles.
- **One photo at a time, blending** into the next on a cream background.
- **Gallery builds**: photos arriving one at a time into a spaced arrangement. Tall photos sit side by side and fill left to right or right to left; wide photos stack and fill top to bottom or bottom to top. Equal spacing throughout.
- **Arrangements that use the photos' shapes cleverly.** A plain grid repeated twice reads as "too simple and repetitive".
- **Following the music closely**: on the beat, then onto a held vocal or the melody when it comes in. Picking up speed when the song does.
- **A brisk pace with few pauses.** "Less pauses in between pics."
- **Starting and ending on a single centred photo** in overlapping styles.
- **Several versions per board** in clearly different styles or backgrounds.

## Rejected

- **Text baked into the edit.** A serif title was "ugly, the opposite of chic". Handwritten captions on polaroids were "not trendy". No titles, captions or labels in the artwork unless asked. Hook text is added in TikTok when posting, not rendered here.
- **Anything that feels animated.** Photos dropping, sliding or settling into place; wipes (a soft pale-blue wipe was "awful"); drifting snow. Photos appear in place and stay put.
- **Jarring transitions.** A quick blend on every fast beat strobes. Smooth means a slower pace, an eased blend of about 0.5 s or a soft in-place fade of about 0.25 s, each finishing on the beat.
- **Cropped or half-shown photos**, photos running off the screen, a photo split across two spaces.
- **A photo appearing small and then growing**, or a new photo smaller than the one it covers.
- **Off-centre or lopsided placement**, uneven gaps, big empty patches.
- **White edges showing inside a photo's frame.** Crop a junk white print border off so the border is only the one the edit draws.
- **Thick borders.**
- **Red photo borders.** Use white, off-white or cream.
- **One photo dwarfing the others on the opening slide.** Keep opening sizes similar.
- **A blank background between slides** in full-screen and collage styles. Go straight to the next slide unless a clear is part of the design.
- **Two photos of the same kind next to each other** (two dresses, two party scenes, two seascapes).
- **Repeated photos**, unless a loop or second pass is asked for.
- **Previews that have to be downloaded to play.** They must play in the viewing page on a phone.

## How the user gives direction

- They often name a background hex, a style ("overlapping", "one at a time", "blending"), a pace ("faster", "slow it down a little") and one or two anchors ("McKenna Grace first", "keep the mockingbird dress in the middle").
- "You have creative freedom" still means: photos centred, borders thin, rules kept.
- They react to results quickly and bluntly. Take the reaction literally, change exactly what was named, and keep what they said was good.
- When they ask for a swap, replace the photo with one from the same board.
- They have asked for longer edits several times. Make what they ask, tell them once what length costs in watch time, and include a short cut of the same edit alongside it.
