# TikTok Edit Rules

Source of truth: the "TikTok Edit Rules" doc, https://claude.ai/artifact/Tcxv4g9jUSAUP7XoegQuDg. If the user says the rules changed, re-sync this file from that doc.

## Purpose

Every edit must look intentional, polished and different from the last one: these rules are the floor, the chosen style is the direction. They apply to any edit, any song and any set of photos, starting from a fresh session with no other context.

- **Hard rules** (Never do, Layout) are never broken, even if a layout would fill more of the screen.
- **Judgment rules** (Order, Variety, Transitions) are how to make an edit that stops the scroll and keeps people watching.
- When the user's instruction for a specific edit conflicts with a default here, the user's instruction wins for that edit. Ask before breaking a hard rule.
- Never show the user anything that has not passed the QA checklist.

## Never do: photos

Photos are shown whole, sharp and exactly as the user chose them.

1. **Never cut a photo off.** No cropping to fill a box, no splitting a photo across two spaces, no letting it run off the screen edge. The only allowed crop removes a junk border or watermark (a white print border, a site watermark), and it is noted in the config.
2. **Never stretch or squash.** Every photo keeps its own width-to-height shape.
3. **Never invent images.** Use only photos from the user's folder, their uploads or their own Pinterest boards. No AI-generated images, no stock photos, unless the user asks.
4. **Never repeat a photo** in one edit, unless the user asks for a loop or repeat.
5. **Never move a photo once it lands.** No zoom, pan or drift on a still photo. It appears, holds, and leaves.
6. **Never pop in small and then grow.** A photo appears at its final size in one action, not two.
7. **Never overlap photos in a gallery layout.** In a stacked collage a cover may sit on top, but only if it is at least as big as the photo it covers.
8. **Never identify a person from their face.** Use the board's captions, file names or the user's word, and ask the user when unsure.
9. **Never sync to anything that is not the song.** Screen recordings end in TikTok's outro jingle, control centre or silence; the edit ends where the music ends.

## Layout and spacing

A slide should look designed, not dropped: spacing that makes sense, balanced sizes, and photos as big as the screen allows.

- **Spacing that makes sense.** Gaps don't have to be one fixed number, but they must look deliberate: consistent within a slide, or varied on purpose (for example a wider gap that separates two clear groups). Never uneven by accident.
- **No wasted screen.** Spread photos out or grow them rather than leaving big empty patches. Empty background is only fine when it is clearly part of the design.
- **Clean edges.** Photos that sit side by side line up top and bottom; photos stacked in a column line up left and right. The overall block reads as a tidy shape and sits balanced on the screen, never crammed against one edge.
- **Group by shape.** Tall photos sit side by side in rows and fill left to right or right to left. Wide photos stack in a column and fill top to bottom or bottom to top.
- **Balanced sizes.** No single photo should dwarf the rest unless it is the deliberate hero of the slide. On an opening slide, keep the largest photo within about 1.6 times the smallest. Flag anything that ends up filling half the screen by accident.
- **Make photos big.** Aim for photos to cover most of the screen (roughly 65–90%). A slide under about 60% looks empty.
- **No awkward positioning.** No photos floating at random spots, no lopsided blocks, no slivers of space that are almost but not quite a gap, nothing that just looks off. If it looks awkward, it is wrong even if every other rule passes.
- **Mind the tradeoff.** When a rule (variety, balance) costs screen coverage, keep the rule and tell the user what it cost.

## Timing and sync

Every cut lands on something you can hear, within one frame (1/30 s); the music decides the pace, not a timer.

- **Listen first.** Before planning cuts, map the track: tempo, downbeats, where the full band or melody comes in, the big hits, the quiet parts, vocal moments. Screenshot or listen to the user's reference clip to confirm.
- **Follow what the song is doing.** On the beat for the rhythmic parts; on the vocal (a held note, a vibrato) when the singer takes over; on brass or melody hits when that's the hook. A steady one-photo-per-beat grid is the fallback, not the goal.
- **Pace to the song and the ask.** Half-beat cuts for a fast, upbeat track; one per beat or slower for a grand or slow one. Quiet intros get a slower start; busy runs get quick bursts.
- **Hold the finished slide.** After a fast run, let the complete slide sit for a moment so people can take it in, then clear.
- **Big hits get big moments.** Start a new slide or land the hero photo on the strongest hits. Never clear the screen on the song's biggest hit unless that is the effect.
- **End with the song.** Measure where the music ends in the recording and stop there. Never time cuts to TikTok's outro jingle, silence or a screen recording's end.
- **Frame zero = sound zero.** The first photo is on screen at 0.00 s; the audio is never offset from the video.

## Transitions

Transitions should feel chic and smooth: clean, purposeful moves that match the song, never effects for their own sake.

- **Default is the hard cut.** Photos appear and disappear in one frame, on the beat. Use other transitions only when the user asks for them or the chosen style calls for them.
- **When transitions are asked for, make them smooth.** A short cross-dissolve, a clean cover (a new photo lands over the last at the same size or larger), a swap in place, or a clear back to the background. Each one is timed so it finishes on the musical event.
- **Don't repeat the same transition back to back** when the style uses several kinds.
- **No cheap moves.** No pops, bounces, spins, shakes, zoom punches or flashy wipes unless the user asks for that look.
- **A slide ending is a transition too.** Clearing to the plain background between groups gives the eye a reset; place clears on weaker beats so the next slide can start on a strong one.

## Order and engagement

The order should keep someone watching to the last frame and make them want to replay, save or comment.

1. **Hook in the first second.** Open on the most recognisable or striking photo (a person the user asks for, the strongest image in the set). The first frame is what people see while scrolling.
2. **Build, don't front-load.** Save a showstopper for the biggest hit near the end. Mid-edit slides should keep surprising: new shapes, new colours, new subjects.
3. **Contrast keeps eyes moving.** Alternate close-ups with full looks, dark with light, people with objects or places, crowds with single subjects.
4. **Tell a small story when the theme allows.** Group photos so each slide reads as a moment or scene, then move to the next.
5. **Make it loop.** End on a bar line and on a photo that leads naturally back to the first one, so a replay feels seamless and adds watch time.
6. **Reward a pause.** Hold finished slides long enough to screenshot; people who stop to look are the ones who save and share.
7. **Follow the user's anchors.** If they say who goes first or what stays in a certain spot, that is fixed; everything else is yours to arrange for the best flow.

## Variety and curation

No two slides should feel the same, and no two similar photos should sit side by side.

- **Tag every photo by kind** before laying out (for example: outfit, crowd or party scene, portrait, beauty close-up, object, place). Two photos of the same kind never touch.
- **Vary the arrangement every slide.** Don't repeat a plain grid, and don't reuse the final slide's shape right before it. Shapes to draw from: zigzag (big photo beside a stacked pair, then mirrored), T (big photo plus a stacked pair over a row of three), sidebar (big photos beside a strip of smaller ones), staggered columns, pair-big-pair.
- **Use each photo's shape creatively.** Tall, narrow photos make strong heroes beside a stacked pair; squares stack neatly; wide photos anchor the top or bottom.
- **Use the whole set when asked,** and keep any photo the user calls out. When removing a photo, replace it with one from the same source unless told otherwise.
- **Carry user-given patterns all the way through.** If the user sets a pattern (for example alternating photos with and without a certain element), keep it on every step, including back around the loop.
- **Check image quality.** Flag low-resolution sources (under about 500 px) that will look soft at the size they're shown.

## Styles that worked

These are proven starting points; build on them and mix them, don't copy them.

| Style | Look | Pace |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gallery build | Photos appear one at a time on a solid background, well spaced, never overlapping; the slide clears and the next shape starts | Half-beat for fast songs, on the hits for grand or slow songs |
| Stacked collage | One centred photo at a time on a soft background; swaps and same-size-or-larger covers, never smaller | On the melody pulse |
| Fast full-screen cut | Each photo fills the frame, hard cuts | Every beat or half-beat |
| Vocal follow | Starts on the beat, switches to the singer's held notes or vibrato, then lands the key word | Beat, then voice |

One light, consistent colour grade and one fixed grain layer across the whole edit, so photos from different sources sit together.

## Workflow and QA

Nothing goes to the user until every check below passes on the rendered video file.

1. Gather photos from the user's folder, uploads or board (Pinterest board feed: `pinterest.com/<user>/<board>.rss`), saved with descriptive file names.
2. Extract the audio from the reference recording; find where the song ends.
3. Map the music: tempo, downbeats, hits, melody and vocal entries.
4. Tag each photo (kind, shape, quality) and note the user's fixed points.
5. Plan slides and order, then search layouts for the biggest photos that obey every rule.
6. Render, then run QA on the file:
   - [ ] Every cut within 1 frame of its musical event
   - [ ] Held frames are perfectly still
   - [ ] No overlap, no cropping, no stretching, no repeats
   - [ ] Spacing looks deliberate, no excess empty screen, nothing awkward
   - [ ] No two same-kind photos touching
   - [ ] Screen coverage reported per slide
   - [ ] Ends with the song, loops cleanly
7. Look at the sequence sheet yourself; fix anything awkward before sending.
8. Report in plain words what changed, what each slide is, and any tradeoff or guess (such as who is in a photo).

## Deliverables

Each edit ships as a viewable video plus everything needed to remake it.

- **Video:** 1080 × 1920, 30 fps, H.264, with the song; plus a silent copy for posting with TikTok's in-app sound.
- **Preview:** a lighter 720p copy on the shared viewing page, playable in the browser and on a phone without downloading first, with a Save button.
- **Sequence sheet:** one image showing every step with its time, for a quick review.
- **QA report** with the results of every check.
- **Reproducible:** one script plus one config file per edit, so any version can be re-rendered or tweaked. New versions are alternatives, not replacements, unless the user says to change one in place.
- **Captions and hashtags** when the user asks for them.

## Tools in this repo

- `gallery.py --config <file>`: gallery-build edits. A config's `groups` list one slide each: `images`, a layout `tree` (`["h", ...]` side by side, `["v", ...]` stacked, numbers = photo index) or a `flow` (ltr/rtl/ttb/btt), optional `order`, `at` (hit times in seconds) and `clear_at`. `categories` enables the same-kind-touching check. Renders the MP4, silent copy, QA report and sequence sheet into `out/<name>/`.
- `collage.py`: stacked-collage edits (swaps and covers). `moodboard.py`: full-screen cut edits and the shared audio analysis (beats, onsets).
- `images/`: source photos. `audio/`: song files (git-ignored; extract them from the user's screen recording with ffmpeg).
- Shared viewing page for previews: https://claude.ai/artifact/BMttNKuh7vvfpkbwuHYeKq
